Schengen Visa Cover Letter Template for Pakistani Applicants (2026)

Last updated: 25 June 2026

By Al Bari Travel & Tours · 24 June 2026 · 3 min read

The cover letter is one of the most underrated documents in a Schengen visa application. Pakistani applicants who skip it or submit a generic template often face rejection. A strong cover letter addresses each consular concern directly, references your supporting evidence, and demonstrates clear intent to return to Pakistan. This page provides a customisable template and explains exactly what to include.

What a Schengen cover letter must achieve

  1. Establish travel purpose — tourism, family visit, business meeting, conference attendance
  2. Confirm specific itinerary — cities, dates, accommodation, return flight
  3. Demonstrate financial capacity — reference your bank statements, employment, source of funds
  4. Establish strong ties to Pakistan — the most critical element for Pakistani applicants (employment, family, property, ongoing studies, business obligations)
  5. Confirm intent to return — explicitly state return plan with date
  6. Reference all supporting documents by name

Template (customise the highlighted sections)

[Your full name as on passport]
[Your CNIC number]
[Your home address in Pakistan]
[Your contact email and phone number]
[Today's date]

The Consul General
[Consulate name — e.g. Embassy of France in Islamabad]
[Address]

Subject: Schengen Visa Application — [Your name] — [Passport number]

Dear Sir / Madam,

I am writing in support of my Schengen visa application to visit [country/countries] from [departure date] to [return date], with the purpose of [tourism / family visit / business meeting / conference].

Travel itinerary:

  • [Date]: Arrival in [city], staying at [hotel name]
  • [Date]: Visit to [city/attraction]
  • [Date]: Travel to [next city] for [purpose]
  • [Date]: Departure from [city] returning to [Pakistan city]

Financial responsibility: I will fully fund this trip myself / my [relationship] is sponsoring this trip. My bank account at [Pakistani bank name] holds [PKR amount] as shown in the attached 6-month bank statement (Document 4 in the file). Estimated trip cost is approximately EUR [amount], covered by these funds plus my current employment salary of PKR [monthly salary] per month at [employer name] (Document 5).

Ties to Pakistan: I am [a permanent employee at / business owner of / a registered student at] [organisation name] since [start date] (employment letter / business registration attached as Document 6). My spouse [name] and our [number] children [names + ages] reside with me at the address above (NADRA family documents attached as Document 7). I own [property type] in [city] (property documents attached as Document 8). I have ongoing financial commitments to [insurance / loan / school fees] in Pakistan.

Return intent: I will return to Pakistan on [date] via [airline name, flight number] to resume my work at [employer] / studies at [institution] / family responsibilities. My confirmed return flight booking is attached.

Previous international travel: [List previous visits with year and country — helps establish travel pattern. If first-time international, omit this paragraph or say "This will be my first international trip; I have a clean passport with no prior overstay or rejection."]

Travel insurance: Comprehensive travel insurance covering EUR 30,000 across the Schengen Area for the full trip duration has been purchased from [insurance provider] (Document 9).

All documents listed are attached to this application. I am happy to provide any additional information requested.

Thank you for considering my application.

Sincerely,
[Signature]
[Your full name typed]

Key tips for Pakistani applicants

  • Be specific. "Sightseeing in Europe" is weak. "Visit the Eiffel Tower 12 Aug, Louvre 13 Aug, day-trip to Versailles 14 Aug" is strong.
  • Reference document numbers. Consular officers process applications in batches; they appreciate cover letters that point to specific evidence in the file.
  • One page maximum. Concise wins. If your cover letter spills onto page two, tighten it.
  • English is preferred. All Schengen embassies accept English. Don't submit Urdu unless required.
  • Sign in pen. Print, sign with a real pen, then scan if uploading.
  • Don't over-promise. If you say you'll return on a specific flight, make sure you actually have that booking.
  • Match the consulate's tone. French consulate is more formal than Spanish; German is the most formal.

What NOT to include

  • Emotional appeals or stories of personal hardship
  • Promises you cannot back up
  • Negative comments about previous visa rejections
  • Mention of intent to extend the stay or apply for residence
  • Mention of intent to work abroad

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Schengen cover letter mandatory?

+

Not technically mandatory but strongly recommended, especially for Pakistani applicants who face higher rejection rates (~32% at most consulates in 2024). A well-written cover letter directly addresses consular concerns and significantly improves approval odds.

Can I use the same cover letter for different Schengen countries?

+

Apply at the embassy of the country where you&apos;ll spend the most days. The cover letter should be addressed to that specific consulate and reference its country as your primary destination, even if you&apos;re visiting multiple Schengen countries.

How long should the cover letter be?

+

One page (A4) maximum. Consular officers process thousands of applications &mdash; tight, specific letters get read carefully. Long rambling letters get skimmed.

Should the cover letter be on company letterhead if I am employed?

+

No &mdash; the cover letter is from YOU personally. Your employer NOC (separate document) is on company letterhead. The cover letter is on plain paper or your own personal letterhead.

Do I need to print the cover letter in colour?

+

Black-and-white print is fine. What matters is content and clarity, not visual presentation.

Can someone else write the cover letter for me?

+

Anyone can draft it, but it must be signed by you personally and must reflect your circumstances accurately. Consular interviews sometimes test whether you actually understand what you submitted.
