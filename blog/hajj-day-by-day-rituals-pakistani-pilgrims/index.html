Hajj Day-by-Day Rituals: A Pakistani Pilgrim's Guide

Last updated: 25 June 2026

By Haiwad Ahmad · 24 May 2026 · 13 min read

The Hajj rites condense into just 5–6 days of the year — 8 to 13 Dhu al-Hijjah — but they include the most spiritually charged moments of any Muslim's life. This guide walks Pakistani pilgrims through each day, what your group leader should be doing, and the small mistakes that cause stress or fidya (penalty).

The night before — 7 Dhu al-Hijjah

You've already arrived in Makkah from Madinah days or weeks earlier. By now you've performed Umrah of Tamattu (if you're a Tamattu pilgrim — the most common type for Pakistanis). On the evening of 7 Dhu al-Hijjah, your group will brief you on the next day's schedule. Sleep early — you'll need the rest.

Day 1 — 8 Dhu al-Hijjah (Yawm at-Tarwiyah)

  1. After Fajr, perform Ghusl (full ritual bath) and put on Ihram (men: 2 white cloths; women: modest stitched clothing).
  2. Declare the Niyyah for Hajj: Labbayk Allahumma Hajjan
  3. Recite the Talbiyah loudly (men) or quietly (women): Labbayk Allahumma labbayk…
  4. Travel to Mina by bus (Pakistani groups depart in waves from 6 AM onwards). The 8 km journey can take 1–4 hours depending on traffic.
  5. Arrive at your assigned Pakistani tent in Mina. Identify the tent number and group leader's location.
  6. Spend the day in worship, dua, and Qur'an recitation. Pray Zuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha, and Fajr (of 9 Dhu al-Hijjah) shortened (qasr) but not combined.
  7. Avoid leaving the Pakistani tent area — getting lost in Mina without your group is a real risk.

Day 2 — 9 Dhu al-Hijjah (Yawm Arafah — The Day of Hajj)

This is THE day. The Prophet ﷺ said: 'Al-Hajju Arafah' — Hajj IS Arafah. Standing on Arafah from Zawal (just after noon) to sunset is the essential pillar of Hajj. If you miss this, you have not performed Hajj.

  1. After Fajr in Mina, travel by bus to Arafah (15–20 km, typically 1–3 hours in heavy traffic). Some Pakistani groups now depart before Fajr to reach Arafah early.
  2. Settle in your Arafah tent. Pray Zuhr and Asr together in shortened form (jam' and qasr) at the time of Zuhr — this is a strong Sunnah.
  3. From Zawal to sunset, stand in continuous dua. This is the most rewarded dua of the year. Face the Qiblah. Be humble. Cry. Ask for everything — for yourself, your family, the Ummah.
  4. The Prophet ﷺ said the best dua is: La ilaha illa Allah wahdahu la sharika lah, lahul-mulku wa lahul-hamd wa huwa 'ala kulli shay'in qadeer.
  5. After sunset, do NOT pray Maghrib. Board your bus to Muzdalifah immediately.

Day 2 (continued) — Night of 9–10 Dhu al-Hijjah (Muzdalifah)

  1. Arrive at Muzdalifah (~10 km from Arafah, can take 2–5 hours of bus crawl).
  2. Pray Maghrib and Isha together (combined) on arrival.
  3. Sleep on the open ground. There are no tents in Muzdalifah — pilgrims sleep under the stars on prayer mats.
  4. Collect 49–70 pebbles (small stones, slightly larger than a chickpea) for the stoning ritual of the next days.
  5. Stay until at least after Fajr. Elderly, women, and weak pilgrims may leave after midnight per Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.

Day 3 — 10 Dhu al-Hijjah (Yawm an-Nahr / Eid al-Adha)

The busiest single day of Hajj. Five separate rites all happen between dawn and night.

  1. Stone Jamarat al-Aqaba — the largest of the three pillars. Throw 7 pebbles, one at a time, reciting Bismillah Allahu Akbar with each.
  2. Qurbani (sacrifice) — most Pakistani pilgrims pay for the qurbani electronically via the Saudi Adahi system. Receipt is issued; you no longer need to be physically present at the slaughterhouse.
  3. Halq or Taqsir — men shave the head (preferred) or trim. Women trim a fingertip's length.
  4. Exit Ihram (Tahallul al-Awwal) — after Halq/Taqsir, you exit Ihram. Most restrictions are lifted EXCEPT intimate relations with spouse.
  5. Tawaf al-Ifadah — return to Makkah and perform 7 laps of Tawaf at the Kaabah, followed by 7 laps of Sa'i. After this, all Ihram restrictions are lifted (Tahallul ath-Thani).
  6. Return to Mina by Maghrib if possible.

Day 4 — 11 Dhu al-Hijjah (First Day of Tashreeq)

  1. Spend the day and night in Mina.
  2. After Zawal (just past noon), stone all three Jamarat in order: small (Jamarat al-Sughra), middle (Jamarat al-Wusta), large (Jamarat al-Kubra/Aqaba). 7 pebbles each, 21 total.
  3. Make dua at the small and middle Jamarat after stoning. No dua at the large Jamarat (Sunnah).
  4. Sleep in Mina.

Day 5 — 12 Dhu al-Hijjah (Second Day of Tashreeq)

  1. Same as Day 4: stone all three Jamarat after Zawal.
  2. Pilgrims who wish to leave Mina early (Ta'ajjul) may depart before Maghrib of 12 Dhu al-Hijjah — but you MUST leave before sunset, otherwise you must stay for Day 6.
  3. Pilgrims staying for Day 6 spend a third night in Mina.

Day 6 (optional) — 13 Dhu al-Hijjah (Third Day of Tashreeq)

  1. For pilgrims who stayed in Mina, stone all three Jamarat one final time after Zawal.
  2. Then return to Makkah.

Final Rite — Tawaf al-Wada (Farewell Tawaf)

Before leaving Makkah for Pakistan, every Hajj pilgrim must perform Tawaf al-Wada — 7 laps of Tawaf at the Kaabah. This is wajib (obligatory). Menstruating women are exempted.

Common mistakes Pakistani pilgrims make

  • Not realising shortened (qasr) prayer in Mina is for travellers — Saudi residents pray full
  • Missing the Arafah window by arriving after Zawal or leaving before sunset
  • Praying Maghrib in Arafah (forbidden — pray it combined with Isha in Muzdalifah)
  • Stoning before Zawal on the days of Tashreeq (invalid — must be after Zawal)
  • Departing Muzdalifah before midnight without a valid Sunnah excuse
  • Forgetting to perform Tawaf al-Ifadah before leaving Makkah — this is a pillar of Hajj
  • Stoning all 21 pebbles into one Jamarat instead of 7 each

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most important day of Hajj?

+

9 Dhu al-Hijjah — the Day of Arafah. The Prophet ﷺ said: 'Hajj is Arafah.' Standing in the plain of Arafah from Zawal to sunset on this day is the essential pillar without which Hajj is not valid.

Can I leave Muzdalifah early on the night of 9–10 Dhu al-Hijjah?

+

Yes, for the elderly, women, weak pilgrims, and those caring for them. The Prophet ﷺ permitted these groups to leave after midnight. For other pilgrims, staying until after Fajr is recommended.

How do I know how many pebbles to collect at Muzdalifah?

+

49 pebbles minimum (7 on 10 Dhu al-Hijjah at Jamarat al-Aqaba + 21 each on 11 and 12 Dhu al-Hijjah). If staying for 13 Dhu al-Hijjah, collect 70. Most pilgrims collect 70 to be safe — extra pebbles are simply unused.

Is qurbani included in Pakistani Hajj packages?

+

Most reputable Pakistani Hajj packages include the qurbani fee. The pilgrim signs up via the Saudi Adahi (sacrifice voucher) system electronically, and a receipt is issued. Verify with your operator at booking that this is included — some basic packages exclude it.

What happens if I miss the Tawaf al-Ifadah on 10 Dhu al-Hijjah?

+

You can perform Tawaf al-Ifadah any time before leaving Makkah. Many Pakistani pilgrims perform it on 11 or 12 Dhu al-Hijjah due to crowd congestion on 10 Dhu al-Hijjah. However, until you perform Tawaf al-Ifadah, intimate relations with spouse remain prohibited.

Can I perform Hajj without ever doing Sa'i if I already did it during my Umrah of Tamattu?

+

For Tamattu pilgrims (the most common Pakistani choice), Sa'i is performed twice — once with Umrah and once with Hajj (after Tawaf al-Ifadah). For Qiran pilgrims, one Sa'i suffices. For Ifrad pilgrims, the Sa'i during Tawaf al-Ifadah is sufficient. Confirm your Hajj type with your group leader.
